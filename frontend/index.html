The model

Not a tool you buy. Someone who deploys

MARK doesn’t sell you software and wish you luck. A production creative embeds with your department, runs a real job alongside your team, and leaves behind a system built on your rate cards, your vendors and your crew — not a generic template.

What we believe →
01

Embedded, not licensed

A line producer or 1st AD who has done the job at scale sits inside your production department for the length of the engagement. Their judgement is the product; the AI removes the work nobody wanted anyway.

02

Built on your data

Your rate cards, your vendor list, your crew roster, your last three cost reports. The system knows what a shoot day actually costs you — not what it costs on average.

03

Agentic search

Rate cards, union schedules, vendor catalogues, permits, weather windows. The answer a producer would have spent half a day finding, with its sources attached.

04

Trained on outcomes

What came in under. What overran. Which line the producer cut, and which vendor delivered. Every production that runs through MARK sharpens the estimate on the next one.

How it works

Slate. Wire. Ship. With a human in the cut

The architecture, and why →
01

Slate the production

Drop in the script, the dates, the lead cast — or just paste a paragraph. The agentic layer sluglines the scenes, counts the days, builds the breakdown. The line producer on duty checks it before you see it.

02

Wire the floor

Plug in Gmail, WhatsApp, Drive. Crew, vendors, location managers, NDAs — MARK holds the rolodex and the paper trail. Our 1st AD on the module makes sure the call sheet reads like a call sheet, not a SaaS export.

03

Ask, and it ships

“Cut tomorrow’s call sheet.” “Trim catering by ₹2L.” “Send the new SOC to all 40 crew.” English or Hindi. The AI does the busywork; the human reviews anything you’d want a human to review.
