# Pricing basis

Why each stage is priced where it is. Keep this to hand — when a client
pushes on fee, the answer is a comparable, not a discount.

All fees in **USD**. Entity is UK (THE MARIO JUDE LTD), so see §5 on
currency and §6 on VAT before issuing.

---

## 1. Stage 0 — Teardown · $4,500 fixed

| Comparable | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Independent consultant day rate, mid-level | $900–$1,500/day → **$4,500–$7,500** for 5 days | ConsultFees 2026 benchmarks |
| IT/mid-size discovery engagement, fixed fee | $3,000–$8,000 | NMS Consulting |
| Strategy discovery engagement | $5,000–$15,000 | ConsultFees |
| US management consultant average day rate | ~$1,800/day → $9,000 for 5 days | ConsultFees |

**$4,500 is the floor of the discovery band and exactly 5 × the low end of
the independent day rate.** Priced to be approved without a committee. It is
deliberately not priced on value — a teardown that finds a 6-figure annual
leak is worth many multiples of this, and that gap is the argument for Stage 1.

Do not discount it. If a client will not pay $4,500 to find out what their
paperwork costs them, they will not pay for Stage 2.

## 2. Stage 1 — Bootcamp · from $18,000

| Comparable | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Line producer fee, % of project budget | **10–15%** of total | EditFX / industry standard |
| Independent consultant, 3 working weeks | $13,500–$22,500 (15 days @ $900–$1,500) | ConsultFees |
| Boutique firm blended team rate | $3,000–$6,000/day | ConsultFees |
| AI consulting paid discovery phase | $20,000–$50,000 | Groovy Web 2026 |

**$18,000 lands under a line producer's 10–15% on any production above
~$180k**, while covering budget, call sheets, cost reporting and system
configuration — more than an LP delivers. That comparison is the pitch:
*"below what you already pay a line producer, and you keep the system."*

Below ~$120k production budget the percentage argument inverts and Stage 1
looks expensive. For those, sell Stage 0 and stop.

**$9,000/month sits mid-band in every source.** Six-month minimum makes the

Scale up, not down: a client running 40 productions a year should be at the
top of the band or on a per-production uplift.

## 3b. India — the same ladder, priced in rupees

Adopted 4 September 2026. The USD ladder holds for the UK, the US and for
Indian **studios**; the rupee band exists for the segment below them.

$4,500 is roughly ₹4 lakh. To Applause, Banijay Asia or Balaji that is a
rounding error. To a seventeen-person ad-film house turning over ₹5–10 crore it
is a decision that leaves the producer's desk, goes to the owner, and stalls —
and a stalled Stage 0 is a lost Stage 1, because the whole point of the fee is
that it is small enough to be decided by one person in one meeting.

| Segment | Stage 0 | Stage 1 | Stage 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad-film / TVC house (10–60 staff) | **₹1,50,000** | ₹6–8 L | ₹1.5–2.5 L/month |
| Branded-content studio | **₹1,50,000** | ₹6–8 L | ₹1.5–2.5 L/month |
| Line-production / service company | **₹1,50,000** | ₹6–8 L | ₹1.5–2.5 L/month |
| OTT / film studio, TV factory | keep the USD ladder — $4,500 / $18,000 / $9,000 per month | | |

**Why ₹1,50,000 and not ₹4 lakh.** The comparable is not a US consultant's day
rate, it is what the same five days costs the client to *not* buy: an Indian
line producer runs ₹25,000 a day, so five days of a senior pair of eyes is
₹1.25 lakh at market. ₹1.5 lakh is that, plus the ledger and the readout. It is
also under the ₹2 lakh mark where most Indian production houses require a second
signature. Both of those are the argument — not the discount.

**Do not translate the USD number.** Quote ₹1,50,000 as the price, never
"$4,500, which is about ₹4 lakh". The second version invites a currency
conversation and reads as a foreign firm's rate card applied to an Indian client.

### Mechanics that are not optional in India

1. **Invoice in INR to an Indian entity.** A USD invoice adds an FX conversation
   and a finance-team delay, and can add FEMA questions for the client's
   accountant. Quote and settle in rupees.
2. **100% upfront below ₹2 lakh.** Indian production payment cycles run 60–90
   days. A 50/50 split on ₹1.5 lakh means chasing ₹75,000 for three months; the
   chase costs more than the split protects.
3. **GST.** THE MARIO JUDE LTD is a UK company with no Indian establishment, so
   this is an import of service and the Indian client accounts for GST under
   reverse charge at 18%. Say so on the invoice — "GST payable by the recipient
   under reverse charge" — and do not add GST to the total yourself. Confirm
   with the accountant before the first Indian invoice; the alternative (being
   treated as having a place of business in India) is a different regime
   entirely.
4. **TDS will be deducted from you.** An Indian company paying a foreign
   supplier for professional services withholds under s.195. Expect the receipt
   to be short by the applicable rate, take the withholding certificate, and
   claim it under the UK–India DTAA. Price with that in mind: ₹1,50,000 invoiced
   is not ₹1,50,000 received.

Points 3 and 4 are the same open question as the compliance engine — they need
the same accountant, in the same conversation.

## 4. Stage 3 — Platform · scoped

No external comparable — this is priced against the Stage 2 retainer the
client is already paying. Rule of thumb: convert at 40–60% of the Stage 2
monthly, since intensity drops but the system stays load-bearing. Never
price Stage 3 before Stage 2 has run six months; you will underprice it.

## 5. Currency — this is a real problem, not a footnote

Fees are quoted in USD. The receiving account is a UK Tide business account.

**Tide cannot hold foreign-currency balances.** Incoming USD (via ACH) and
30+ currencies (via SWIFT) are auto-converted to GBP at Tide's rate, and that
rate carries a markup that does not appear as a line item on the statement.
On an $18,000 invoice a 1.5–2% spread is $270–$360, invisible.

Options, in order of preference:

1. **Wise Business or a USD-capable account** for USD invoices — hold USD,
   convert when the rate suits. Settle GBP invoices into Tide as now.
2. **Quote USD, invoice and settle in GBP** at a rate fixed on the invoice
   date, stated on the invoice. Simple; client carries no FX risk; you carry
   none either.
3. **Tide as-is** — acceptable for UK clients paying GBP. Leaky for USD.

Also note **card fees**: Tide Payment Links take Visa/Mastercard and
Apple/Google Pay, minimum £5, settling in 2–3 working days (next day for
£2.99+VAT/month). Card acceptance on an $18,000 invoice costs real money —
use the payment link for Stage 0 convenience and **bank transfer for Stage 1
and above.**

## 6. VAT

THE MARIO JUDE LTD is a UK company. The VAT registration threshold is £90,000
of taxable turnover in a rolling 12 months.

- **Below threshold and not registered:** charge no VAT. Do not put a VAT line
  on the invoice, and do not show a VAT number you do not have.
- **Registered:** UK B2B clients are charged VAT at the standard rate.
  Services to overseas business clients are generally outside the scope of UK
  VAT under the place-of-supply rules (reverse charge) — but the treatment
  depends on the client's status and location.

Three of the four stages will take you past £90k with two clients. Confirm
your current registration status with an accountant before the first invoice,
not after.
